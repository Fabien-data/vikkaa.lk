# Vikkaa.lk: Court Brief and Foundation Plan

22 November 2026 · For the two co-founders

This brief has two parts.

- **Part A is the court's verdict.** Twelve independent jurors (an accountant, a first-time seller, a competitor, a retired judge, a teenager, an engineer, a small-business owner, a statistician, a parent, an investor, a designer and a lawyer) heard a prosecution and a defense, then voted without seeing each other. Their words are quoted unchanged.
- **Part B is the plan built from that verdict.** It is my analysis, not the jury's. It turns their conditions into a foundation, a 90-day schedule, and a trust, brand, community and international plan.

No outside market research was done for either part. Everything comes from your design document, your answers on 22 November, and the trial record.

---

## Part A: The verdict

### GUILTY, 12 to 0: the case fails as stated

**The case on trial:** "Two co-founders with zero capital will make Vikkaa.lk the best property and vehicle advertising/marketing company: one that dominates in Sri Lanka and is capable of serving international markets as well."

The verdict is on that claim **as stated today**. Every juror wrote down what would flip their vote, and those conditions are the plan in Part B.

### Charges that stuck (the judge's words)

1. **No cash before the money runs out (7 jurors).** Nobody has agreed to pay, the upfront fee is "not decided yet", commission waits for a deed signing, and the runway is "under 3 months."
2. **The claim outruns the evidence (6 jurors).** "Dominates in Sri Lanka" and international reach rest on zero clients, sales or audience. The defense conceded they "cannot be defended now."
3. **Too much trust asked by an unknown firm (5 jurors).** An exclusive, an upfront fee, commission "no matter who found the buyer" and a 6-month tail, with no sales or case study.

### What the defense got right

1. It is a service, not a marketplace: one signed seller is already a live campaign. That fixes the first attempt's failure, as the prosecution agreed.
2. It fits zero capital: owned gear, client-funded ads, and a fee or dealer retainer that is paid before any sale.

### Conditions for acquittal

1. **Set the price and sell it (8 jurors).** Fix the fee and retainer this week, then pitch dealers and developers until one signs and pays before the runway ends.
2. **Shrink the claim (8 jurors).** Make one offer: done-for-you property sales in the Western Province. Drop dominance and international.
3. **Get the contract drafted (4 jurors).** Brief a lawyer now on the exclusive, the tail, ad-deposit refunds, and the fee if nothing sells. Decide who pays the lawyer; the record never says.

### The sentence

> Stop building; start selling. Set your price, sign one paying client under a real contract, and claim no more than you can prove.

### The most telling lines from the jury

- **The Competitor:** "Their edge is drone footage and editing, which I can hire in a week. What I actually have, trust and a known name, is the thing they have none of."
- **The First-Time Customer:** "I would not pay an upfront fee and an ad deposit … sign a 60–90 day exclusive … to a firm that has never sold anything, when I can still list on ikman or use an agent I already know."
- **The Small-Business Owner:** "I have never opened a shop with no price on the shelf and no customer in the door."
- **The Designer:** "A stranger cannot tell in five seconds what Vikkaa is."
- **The Lawyer:** without the contract, "a first client turns into a dispute instead of a case study."

### 2. The success rate: an honest read

There is no honest percentage yet. The Statistician juror put it exactly: every number you would need is zero, and the "top realtors and car dealers" you're comparing yourselves to "only counts the winners."

What can be said:

- **As stated** (dominate Sri Lanka and go international, on under 3 months of runway with zero clients): the court says it fails, and I agree.
- **Narrowed** (done-for-you property sales in the Western Province, plus dealer and developer retainers): it is a real business with a fighting chance, **if and only if one paying client signs before about 31 January 2027.** Every juror's flip condition points at that one event.

**The first real success rate you will have is your pitch-to-paid conversion.** Count every pitch. If 30 pitches to dealers and developers produce 1–2 paying clients, you have a business to grow. If 40 pitches produce none, the offer or the price is wrong, so change it rather than pitch harder.

### 3. SWOT

**Strengths**

- Agency-trained marketing and content skills: premium production that classified ads can't match.
- All the gear is already owned, so there's no capital cost to shoot.
- A live platform that most agents don't have. The client portal, weekly reports and evidence log make transparency visible, and visible transparency is a trust tool.
- It's a service, not a marketplace: one client is already revenue.
- Clients fund their own ads, so you never carry ad spend.
- One founder is an AI engineer, so the Stage 2 WhatsApp assistant can multiply two people's capacity later.
- Focus already chosen: Western Province, property first, and ordinary private cars skipped.

**Weaknesses**

- Zero clients, zero audience, zero track record.
- Under 3 months of runway.
- No price set and no contract drafted.
- Two people do everything, and one of them also writes the software.
- The offer is blurred: "advertising/marketing company" versus an exclusive sales agent.
- A habit of building before selling. The first attempt died of it, and this one has started the same way.

**Opportunities**

- The market is mostly low-quality classified ads. Premium, honest presentation stands out.
- Repeat sellers (dealers, land developers, condo projects) turn one relationship into monthly retainer income.
- Sri Lankans abroad need someone at home they can trust. Your portal, video walkthroughs, remote signing and evidence log are built for exactly that.
- Every shoot is also founder content, so the personal brand gets built as a by-product of the work.
- The Vikkaa Circle buyer list compounds: each campaign makes the next one sell faster.
- The platform itself becomes a sellable product once it's proven on real deals.

**Obstacles (threats)**

- The trust barrier. You're asking for an exclusive, a fee, commission "no matter who found the buyer" and a 6-month tail, from a firm nobody has heard of.
- Incumbents with names. As the Competitor said, your production can be copied in a week, but their trust can't be.
- Cheap alternatives: sellers can list on ikman or call an agent they already know.
- Long sale cycles. Commission only arrives at deed signing or vehicle transfer.
- Legal exposure: the enforceability of the exclusive and the tail, ad-deposit handling, and the PDPA.
- Founder burnout, or one founder taking a job when the money runs out.
- Ops: the platform approvals are pending. The server is shared with other live projects and was about 88% full on 21 Nov.

### 5. Pricing and founding-client terms (decide this week)

The jury pulled in two directions. Eight jurors want a fixed fee that's paid inside the runway. The First-Time Customer wants no upfront risk at all. **Two tracks satisfy both:**

**Track 1: revenue (dealers and developers).** This pays the bills.
- **Dealer retainer:** a fixed monthly fee for N cars a month (shoot, edit, campaign, enquiry handling), paid in advance. Commission is optional per deal.
- **Developer pilot:** a fixed price for one project page or a batch of plots, with one month of campaigning. It's designed to convert into a retainer.
- Paid before work starts. That's the cash inside the runway.

**Track 2: proof (2–3 private property sellers).** This builds the case studies.
- A low or zero upfront fee, commission on sale only, and a shorter exclusive (for example 45–60 days), in exchange for the right to publish the case study and a video testimonial.
- The ad budget is still paid by the client, always.
- Cap it at 3 founding clients, then move to standard terms.

### 7. Trust and personal branding: the realtor and dealer playbook

Top agents and dealers win on three things: **they are visible, they are specific, and they show proof.** You have no proof yet, so lead with visibility and specificity, and let the proof build.

**Trust basics (cheap and done once):**
- A registered business and a bank account in the business name. Never ask for transfers to a personal account.
- One real phone and WhatsApp number, answered fast, every time.
- A Google Business Profile, so reviews start collecting from client one.
- Publish your terms, your ad-deposit refund rule and your "what happens if it doesn't sell" policy on the site. Openness about the hard terms disarms them.
- Show the portal in every pitch: "You'll see every enquiry, every viewing and every rupee of ad spend, live." No agent they know offers that.

**Personal brands: give each founder one lane.**
- **The numbers founder** (the AI engineer): weekly "Western Province price read" videos, area price explainers, and how to price a property right. This builds authority without needing a sales record.
- **The storyteller founder** (the one who runs the social platforms): behind-the-scenes from every shoot, walkthroughs, and the human stories of sellers and buyers.
- Both of you in front of the camera, consistently, under your own names, linked to Vikkaa.

**Content rhythm (made from work you're already doing):**
- 3 posts a week minimum. Every shoot yields at least one founder post.
- A free 10-minute price read as the lead magnet. It's already in your design for no-go sellers. Cap it at a few a week so it doesn't eat selling time.
- One case study per sale, with the seller on video wherever possible.
- **Never post a claim you can't back.** One real result beats ten big promises, and that's the Designer's and the Teenager's point.

### 8. Big clients and networking

- **Start warm.** Your former agency's clients and contacts are the shortest path to a first developer or dealer. Tell every one of them what you do now.
- **Pitch with their own ads.** Print a dealer's or developer's current classified listing next to a Vikkaa-style shot of the same kind of asset. The gap sells itself.
- **Pilot, then retainer.** A fixed-price, one-month pilot is easy to say yes to. Agree the retainer terms in advance, so they apply if the pilot works.
- **Ask every client for two introductions.** Dealers know dealers, and developers know builders. Your design already rewards referrals when the referred client signs.
- **Be present where these people are**: property and vehicle exhibitions, developer launches, dealer associations. Bring the portal demo on a phone.

### 9. Community: the Vikkaa Circle

- Open sign-up now, with recorded consent. The PDPA is a feature here: "We never contact you without permission" is itself a trust message.
- Members see new assets 48 hours early (already designed). Every campaign feeds the list, and the list makes the next campaign sell faster.
- Give members value even when there's nothing to buy: monthly area price reads and buying guides.
- Target from your own design: 1,500 consented buyers by month 6. Track it weekly.

### 10. International: when, not now

- **Not before 3–5 published local case studies.** International trust is borrowed from local proof.
- **First market: Sri Lankans abroad selling property at home.** They need exactly what the platform already does: remote signing, live reports, video walkthroughs, and an evidence log that protects them.
- **Channels:** relatives of local clients, diaspora community groups, and referrals.
- Hold the "international" claim until a real overseas seller has been served.
