## Charges that stuck

1. **No cash before the money runs out (7 jurors).** Nobody has agreed to pay, the upfront fee is "not decided yet", commission waits for a deed signing, and runway is "under 3 months."
2. **The claim outruns the evidence (6 jurors).** "Dominates in Sri Lanka" and international reach rest on zero clients, sales or audience; the defense conceded they "cannot be defended now."
3. **Too much trust asked by an unknown firm (5 jurors).** Exclusive, upfront fee, commission "no matter who found the buyer" and a 6-month tail, with no sales or case study.

## What the defense got right

1. It is a service, not a marketplace: one signed seller is a live campaign. That fixes the first attempt's failure, as the prosecution agreed.
2. It fits zero capital: owned gear, client-funded ads, and a fee or dealer retainer that pays before any sale.

## Conditions for acquittal

1. **Set the price and sell it (8 jurors).** Fix the fee and retainer this week, then pitch dealers and developers until one signs and pays before runway ends.
2. **Shrink the claim (8 jurors).** One offer: done-for-you property sales in the Western Province. Drop dominance and international.
3. **Get the contract drafted (4 jurors).** Brief a lawyer now on the exclusive, the tail, ad-deposit refunds, and the fee if nothing sells. Decide who pays the lawyer; the record never says.

## The sentence

Stop building; start selling. Set your price, sign one paying client under a real contract, and claim no more than you can prove.
